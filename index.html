Real life scenarios

We modelled different life choices to see how they impact lifetime wealth and the prospects of becoming a homeowner. See how small changes make big differences.

James, 21

The "Unwilling Boomerang"

Base outcome: can buy at 38

James moved back in with his parents after graduation. He pays low rent (£800 per month) but spends heavily on his lifestyle (£1,250 per month). He wants to buy a £200k flat.

Key insights from the model:
  • Buying is better: Generates ~£396k more lifetime wealth than renting.
  • Power of family help: A £10k gift from parents cuts 2 years off purchase time and boosts final wealth by £67k.
  • Impact of budgeting: Cutting expenses to £500 per month means he could buy 11 years earlier (at age 27).

Becky, 24

The "Independence Protector"

Base outcome: the rent trap (buy age 46)

Becky values her independence and rents a 1-bed flat alone (£1,150 per month). High rent and lifestyle spending (£1,250 per month) leave little room for saving towards a £160k flat.

Key insights from the model:
  • Wealth depletion: Continuing to rent into retirement drains her lifetime wealth entirely.
  • Compromising on space: Moving to a flatshare (£800 per month) allows her to buy 16 years earlier (at 30).
  • Extreme frugality: Cutting expenses to £500 per month lets her buy at 28, shaving 18 years off her base outcome.

About the report

The Personal Financial Planning Working Party has developed a financial model to examine the acute trade-offs early-career households face between homeownership and pension saving.

Our analysis suggests that, under plausible economic conditions, early entry into homeownership is frequently a superior strategy for building net wealth compared to sustained renting, even with higher pension contributions.

Key drivers identified:

  • Daily spending habits: Discretionary spending significantly impacts when you can afford a deposit.
  • Current rent levels: High rent strengthens the financial case for buying. Low rent makes investing elsewhere more competitive.
  • Family support ("Bank of Mum & Dad"): Cash gifts or reduced rent at home materially alter the timing of a purchase and long-term wealth trajectories.

Note: This model provides a quantitative framework for understanding financial interactions over a lifetime. It is not intended as individual financial or retirement advice.
