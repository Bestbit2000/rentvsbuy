Becky, 24

The "Independence Protector"

"I love having my own space and freedom, but spending half my income on rent just to avoid housemates is starting to feel like a trap. I want my own home, but the math isn't adding up."

The Current Situation

Becky values her independence and currently rents a 1-bedroom flat alone. While she enjoys her solo lifestyle, the combination of high rent and significant discretionary spending leaves little room for saving. Her goal is to buy a £160,000 flat.

Income & Debt

£38,000 salary

£45,000 student loan

Outgoings

£1,150 /mo rent

£1,250 /mo lifestyle

Savings

£1,000 in bank

£2,000 in pension

The Goal

£160,000

1-bed flat

46 Purchase Age

The Base Outcome: The Rent Trap

If Becky changes nothing, the reality is stark. Because her current living expenses consume so much of her income, she will be 46 years old before she can save enough to buy her £160k flat.

Wealth Depletion

If she never buys and continues renting into retirement, her ongoing housing costs will drain her lifetime wealth entirely, leaving her financially vulnerable.

How can Becky escape the rent trap?

To get on the property ladder and protect her financial future, Becky needs to make compromises. Our model tested two main interventions.

Compromising on Space

Becky's biggest expense is her solo rent. Sharing a flat means sacrificing some independence but dramatically altering her financial trajectory.

Scenario A: The Flatshare

Becky moves into a shared house, reducing her rent from £1,150 to £800/mo.

New Purchase Age: 30
Time Saved: 16 Years

By sharing for just a few years, she can afford her own property in exactly 6 years, completely avoiding the retirement wealth trap.

Changing Lifestyle Habits

If Becky refuses to give up her solo flat, her only other lever is her heavy discretionary spending (£1,250 a month).

Scenario B: Extreme Frugality

Keeping the flat, but reducing lifestyle and discretionary spending down to £500 a month.

New Purchase Age: 28
Time to buy: Just 4 Years

"Aggressive saving can outpace the financial drag of high rent, but it requires significant lifestyle discipline."

By aggressively cutting back on nights out, clothes, and trips, she buys at 28—shaving 18 years off her base outcome.
